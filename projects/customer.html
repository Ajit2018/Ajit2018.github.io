Business problem

Customers do not buy features, price or brand in isolation — they choose between complete alternatives.

Portfolio and pricing decisions therefore need to understand which attributes create value for which customers, how demand shifts between offers, and what that means for economics.

01

Heterogeneous needs

Different segments value benefits differently; one average customer hides the trade-offs.

02

Cannibalisation

A stronger offer can shift demand from another product rather than create incremental value.

03

Price architecture

Relative prices shape portfolio choice and cross-border movement, not just unit margin.

04

Market context

China and other market analyses required local portfolio and competitive interpretation, not global averages alone.

Decision principle

The analytical goal is not “predict preference” but to support a choice among portfolio, price and market actions with transparent assumptions.
