Industrial simulation portfolio

Real product problems → physics model → engineering decision.

Selected industrial simulation cases from Philips consumer-product development, spanning transient dynamics, CFD, thermal analysis, structural FEA, contact, multiphysics and assembly-process simulation. The cases show how physics-based modelling was used for root-cause analysis, concept selection, virtual prototyping, optimisation and engineering decisions.

TRANSIENT DYNAMICS

Shaver styler — noise root cause

Problem. Operational noise was above the acceptable limit.
Simulation. A full-assembly transient model showed one gear rocking sideways during operation.
Outcome. The rocking motion was identified as the noise source; changes to design and clearance reduced the noise to an acceptable level.

CONTACT / KINEMATICS

Shaver couplings — concept selection

Problem. Which coupling concept should be selected, and how should its geometry be modified for the best motion transfer?
Simulation. CAD concepts were simulated with contact; velocity, acceleration and displacement were evaluated through the motion.
Outcome. The simulations supported selection of the coupling design with the smoothest motion transfer.

POC / MECHANISM

Drive mechanism — concept feasibility

Problem. Would the proposed mechanism achieve the desired function?
Simulation. The CAD mechanism was simulated and its time-dependent velocities, accelerations and displacements were calculated.
Outcome. The virtual model provided the feasibility evidence for a go / no-go decision before detailed realisation.

CFD / THERMAL

Infrared lamp — overheating diagnosis

Problem. The top of the lamp exceeded the allowable temperature limit.
Simulation. A thermal-flow model included the heat source plus conduction, convection and radiation; the flow field and temperature distribution were examined together.
Outcome. The reason for the hotspot was identified, enabling focused investigation of solution directions.

THERMO-MECHANICAL

Coffee-maker heater tube — thermal-stress check

Problem. Could the steel water tube fail because of thermal stresses?
Simulation. A model of the water tube and resistive heating element was built; deformation and stress due to thermal loading were calculated.
Outcome. The calculated stresses in the steel tube were within the failure limits.

MULTIPHYSICS

Kitchen grill plate — deformation reduction

Problem. The grill plate deformed when heated.
Simulation. The model combined temperature loading, convection, conduction, radiation, mechanical stress and deformation.
Outcome. A unique grid pattern on the underside reduced deformation; the modification solved the problem in tests and was realised in the product.

STRUCTURAL FAILURE

Electric toothbrush node spring — failure analysis

Problem. Why was this key spring component failing at a particular location?
Simulation. Stresses in the node spring were calculated under torsional loading.
Outcome. The failure location observed in the physical spring was captured by the simulation.

PRESSURE / FEA

Steam boiler weld — competitive benchmark

Problem. How did the Philips boiler weld compare with competitors, and could the notch initiate a fatigue crack?
Simulation. Boiler cross-sections were scanned and reverse-engineered for FEA; steam-pressure loading was applied and weld stresses were compared across designs.
Outcome. The questioned notch in the Philips geometry was not located in a critical stress area.

THERMAL FUNCTION

Iron baseplate — temperature uniformity

Problem. What heating-element shape, metal-plate design and thermostat location would produce the required uniform soleplate temperature?
Simulation. Heat transfer and heat loss were modelled; geometry and thermostat position could be iterated while monitoring temperature distribution.
Outcome. The study supported selection of the optimum thermal design.

ASSEMBLY / CONTACT

Shaver spider & cutter — assembly process

Problem. Would assembly remain smooth under nominal and extreme tolerance/process conditions, and would post-assembly stresses stay acceptable?
Simulation. The model calculated the assembly process and resulting stresses, repeated across design variants, process settings and tolerances.
Study purpose. Virtual assessment of assembly robustness before relying on physical process trials alone.

Why simulation matters

Simulation can reduce prototype loops by exposing physical drivers early, screening concepts virtually and defining the operating/design envelope before hardware is frozen.

Why product ownership matters

A function developer uses the model as a decision tool, not as an end product. The objective is a robust function that meets requirements and can be handed over for detailed engineering.

Why this profile is unusual

Deep simulation experience can be embedded directly inside function ownership — reducing dependence on a separate CAE service group while still supporting adjacent projects when specialist analysis is needed.

2026 direction · In development

AI-enabled Computational Engineering

The next step is not “AI replacing CFD/FEA.” It is combining validated physics-based simulation with ML and automation to explore more designs, shorten turnaround time and focus expert attention on the decisions that matter.

PROJECT 01 · IN DEVELOPMENT

ML surrogate for simulation DOE

Generate or use a public CFD/thermal dataset, train a surrogate model on solver outputs, quantify prediction error and demonstrate the speed/accuracy trade-off for design exploration.

PROJECT 02 · ROADMAP

Agentic simulation workflow

Automate case generation, meshing/setup steps, parallel runs, convergence checks, result extraction, failed-run handling and engineering reporting while keeping deterministic solver results authoritative.

PROJECT 03 · ROADMAP

Physics-ML / field prediction

Explore neural operators / graph models for pressure, velocity, temperature or stress-field approximation after the scalar-surrogate foundation is validated.
