Business problem

Supply-chain teams do not need more alerts; they need prioritised choices under constraints.

A disruption matters differently depending on inventory cover, alternate sources, demand criticality, lead time, capacity and financial impact.

01

Signal overload

Many exceptions are visible, but not all deserve the same management attention.

02

Interdependence

A local supplier or capacity issue can propagate across products, plants and markets.

03

Competing objectives

Service, inventory, cost, resilience and working capital cannot all be optimised independently.

04

Actionability

The useful output is a ranked set of feasible mitigations with transparent assumptions.

Deterministic core

Inventory positions, lead times, capacities, costs and scenario arithmetic should remain explicit and auditable.

Predictive layer

Forecasting or risk models can improve prioritisation, but only if they beat simple baselines and respect temporal causality.

AI explanation layer

RAG / agents can retrieve policies and explain options, while the quantitative layer remains the source of truth.
