Management conclusion

The architecture should support a relationship manager, not replace one. The next layer is a priority engine that combines outlook, behaviour and explainable change evidence under workload guardrails; RAG and cockpit layers remain planned.

Business problem

A balance dashboard tells you what happened. A relationship manager needs to know what deserves attention now.

Corporate accounts have different operating rhythms. The same low balance or unusual transaction can mean very different things depending on timing, counterparties, recurring flows and the account's own history.

Results

The most important result was learning where automation should stop.

The work preserved weak and negative findings rather than forcing every technique into a success story.

7–30d

Cash-flow outlook

Evaluated causal account-day forecasts against transparent baselines; longer-horizon cumulative flow was more useful than absolute balance targets.

6

Behaviour groups

Interpretable descriptive context for throughput, cash buffer, volatility and burn patterns.

0.85

Hybrid alerts / account / month

Workload became manageable compared with transparent rules alone.

5.62%

Hybrid precision

Still far too weak for autonomous customer action — a crucial design finding.

Forecasting

Prefer interpretable liquidity / drawdown targets and keep simple baselines where they remain competitive.

Clustering

Use groups as contextual descriptions, not permanent customer types; stability was only moderate.

Change detection

Rules had high recall but excessive workload. Isolation Forest controlled workload but missed too much. Hybrid signals remain supporting evidence only.

Technical deep dive

Reproducible engineering first; models second.

The technical design emphasizes traceability, causal features, chronological validation and explicit leakage controls.

API

Canonical capture

Read-only account, balance and paginated activity capture with source IDs, arrays, raw JSON and deterministic activity keys.

SPARK

Bronze / Silver

PySpark normalization, proper dates/decimals, type-specific fields, canonical Parquet and automated quality reporting.

SQL

Reusable account analytics

Flow direction, running balances, concentration, frequency, mutation codes and quality diagnostics.

ML

Causal forecasting

Chronological within-account splits, lagged/rolling features and mandatory mean/median/seasonal baselines.

CTX

Behavioural context

Common-window features, skew transforms, correlation pruning, robust scaling and stability checks.

Δ

Explainable change

Median/MAD rules, percentage change, EWMA shifts, account-relative thresholds and a lightweight Isolation Forest.

Leakage control

Synthetic archetypes and 380 injected-event labels were hidden during feature design, thresholds, model selection and tuning. They were joined only after analytical decisions were frozen.

Responsible AI & evidence boundaries

Be explicit about what is real, what is synthetic, and what remains human.

This case study is designed to demonstrate disciplined reasoning, not to imply production banking deployment.

Quantitative truth

Data pipelines and deterministic / statistical models calculate the facts.

AI role

Planned AI retrieves, summarizes and explains governed outputs; it is not the numerical authority.

Human accountability

The relationship manager reviews evidence, assumptions and uncertainty before deciding whether and how to act.

Claims not made

No AML/fraud model, no autonomous customer decision, no production ABN deployment, and no validated production banking performance.
