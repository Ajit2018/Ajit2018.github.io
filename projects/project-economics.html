Business problem

A product launch or portfolio plan creates value only if the underlying drivers actually materialise.

Headline revenue is not enough. Management needs to understand whether price, volume, mix, feature/configuration adoption, funnel conversion, installation cost and other business-case drivers are tracking to plan — and what to change if they are not.

01

Business-case drift

Launch economics can diverge from the original case as actual price, volume, mix and cost evolve.

02

Portfolio concentration

A small number of SKUs can drive most of the value outcome, making prioritisation more important than broad averages.

03

Market dynamics

Competitor moves, price bands and category shifts can create value decline even when unit volume is still growing.

04

Scenario uncertainty

Forward scenarios are decision aids, not guaranteed outcomes; assumptions and attribution need to remain explicit.

Evidence boundaries

Business-case scale and scenario outputs are not the same as realised personal impact.

Supported here

FP&A Project Lead role, two MR launches, recurring business-case realisation monitoring, authored China value-decline analysis and strongly corroborated scenario / range support.

Not claimed

No claim that the ~€2M estimate was realised, that the ~€300M launch scale was Ajit's revenue impact, or that China scenarios were approved / launched / financially realised.
