Business question

Should we discount more—or target more intelligently?

Decision logic

Separate demand response from contribution economics before expanding treatment.

What it demonstrates

Experimentation, pricing economics, decision framing and executive communication.

Business problem

Discounting can increase conversion while destroying economics.

The decision is not “did bookings rise?” It is “where does incremental demand justify the margin cost?”

Commercial tension

  • Higher conversion can be bought with unnecessary discount.
  • Portfolio averages conceal segment differences.
  • Gross response does not prove incrementality.
  • A single KPI can recommend the wrong rollout.

Management output

  • Treatment/control evidence
  • Booking and margin consequences
  • Target segments and guardrails
  • A defensible rollout choice
Management guardrail

Never optimize conversion alone. Evaluate demand, margin, incrementality and constraints together.

The conclusion is deliberately commercial rather than model-centric.

Business interpretation

A pricing experiment can “win” on conversion and still lose on value. The workbench makes that trade-off visible before rollout.

Executive memo

AI-assisted narrative can explain deterministic outputs; it does not calculate or invent the underlying economics.

Responsible AI & data

Transparent synthetic treatment and deterministic business calculations.

Data boundary

Public hotel-booking demand observations support the demonstration. The pricing treatment is explicitly synthetic so no confidential client data is implied.

AI boundary

Deterministic calculations produce experiment and economic facts. AI may summarize them but is not the source of truth.

Human accountability

Managers see assumptions, response, economics and guardrails before choosing a rollout.

Claim boundary

This is a public decision-science demonstration, not claimed production deployment or client performance.
